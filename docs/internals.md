| Scenes (experimental) | `src/components/scene/` | Opt-in entry point `sayshape/scene` (`sceneComponents`): off unless a host registers them (playground `?scene=1`, MCP `--scene`, and the website's chat page with its model proxy in `worker/`). A scene lays out as one box and draws its shape lines itself each frame, with `t` (seconds) and `SCENE_FUNCTIONS`, which only lines inside a scene can call: `ComponentDef.provides` gives enclosing elements' names and functions to the parser, and `evaluate`/`checkSyntax`/`interpolate` take an optional `ExpressionFunctions` set. `path.ts` draws SVG path data with plain canvas calls. Line style (`line-style.ts`): `cap=` and `join=` on line, path and polygon (round by default, as every other outline), and `round=` on path and polygon, which cuts each corner between straight segments back by the radius (at most half of either leg) and bridges it with a quadratic curve through the corner, leaving curves and the corners next to them as written; a path is pressed along its rounded outline (`flattenPath(d, round)`), a polygon by its points; tests `test/scene-line-style.test.ts`. Depth is roles, never values: `shadow=sm|md|lg` and `glow=true` (in the shape's own color) on shapes and labels, `shine=true` (lit from the upper left, so a circle reads as a ball) on filled shapes; tests `test/scene-effects.test.ts`. A shape, group or use with `on=` is pressable (`interact.ts`): the scene tracks its transform while drawing, records each pressable line's outlines and scope per frame, registers one hit region per line (detail = a key from the line's id and its each copy and use), tests hits exactly in `hitTest`, runs `on=` with the scope it was drawn with and the scene's functions (`PressContext.run(actions, { scope, locate, functions })`; `parseActions` and `ActionHost` take the same functions, so `on="set:sel:{pick(steps, i).id}"` works on a scene line and still warns outside one), lists them as `parts` of a `figure`, and draws hover, focus and `selected=` outlines and `tip=` in theme colors. `on-hover=`/`on-leave=` (`hover.ts`, through the engine's `ComponentDef.hover`, which a finger never fires and which hears a part was left when it stops being drawn) run actions as the mouse enters and leaves a line, along its chain of pressable lines like the DOM's mouseenter; tests `test/scene-review-paths.test.ts`); small phone text is one summary, always kept, so it never pushes other problems past `MAX_DRAWING_PROBLEMS`. `let a={…}` names a value for the lines below it in its block (scene, group, each copy, if block, drawing): the parser reads it as `let name= value=` only inside a scene (elsewhere it's still the code-line error) and checks it in `src/lets.ts` (which lets each line sees, loops between lets, a let read where it doesn't reach), and `drawAll` wraps the block's scope with `letScope` (`lets.ts`), worked out once per `f.time`, with writes to its name going nowhere; without z the old path runs unchanged; tests `test/scene-depth.test.ts`. `reflect=<y>` on group and use (`reflect.ts`, behind the reflect switch of `sceneComponentsWith`; `ripple=` and `fade=` 0 to 1, unlisted, described in reflect's line) draws the contents again into an offscreen buffer (`Frame.offscreen`; none in Node or the drawing check, so nothing is drawn there) at the device scale capped at 2, only the rows a viewer can see, with the fade baked in, then flipped about the line in the parent's coordinates and clipped below it, in one piece or, with ripple and motion on, in at most ~300 shifted strips (`rippleOffset`, whole waves per loop); nothing inside is pressable or reviewed, and buffers are kept per line (4, least recently used) while the names the contents read, their place and the theme stay the same, never when they read `t`; tests `test/scene-reflect.test.ts`. Limits: `MAX_SCENE_SHAPES`, `MAX_SCENE_STEPS`, `MAX_RANGE`, `MAX_USE_DEPTH`. Tests: `test/scene.test.ts`, `test/scene-interact.test.ts`, browser `visual/scene.spec.ts`; real-model scoring `eval/scenes.ts` (`npm run scene-eval`); browser timings `bench/browser/` (`npm run bench:scene-browser`); plan: `docs/plans/interactive-scenes.md`; validation report: `docs/plans/interactive-scenes-validation.md`. |

| Motion | `src/motion.ts`, `src/engine.ts`, `src/component.ts`, `src/parser.ts` | Entrances as roles ([language](language.md#motion)): `enter=fade\|rise\|scale` and `stagger=true` on any element (`MOTION_PROPS`, kept apart from `COMMON_PROPS` so the guide, the docs and the "Known props" message are unchanged; `universalProp` is what the parser looks props up in). The parser warns for an unknown role or a non-boolean stagger (from the prop types), for either prop on an element drawn by its parent or with no box of its own (`drawnByParent`, or `ComponentDef.noBox`: table-column, detail, compare-row, the chart layers, pin, scene shapes; `test/entrance.test.ts` checks every element's example either plays or is reported) or on `each`/`if`, and for `stagger=` on an element that takes no children; a component's own prop of the same name wins. It's one mechanism with the rest of the engine's motion: `motionOn()` (the `animate` option, unless reduced motion, the `reducedMotion` option else the system setting, which now wins over `animate: true` too) decides `ctx.motion`, the plain fade-in (`ctx.appear`, `APPEAR_MS` 240), `ctx.tween`, `ctx.reveal`, the notice fade and entrances. Starts are kept by element id in `MotionStarts` (which also holds the fade-in's, formerly the engine's `seen` map), so they outlive re-parses: a streamed line enters once, lines already drawn never replay, and `setSpec` without `resetState` moves them with their lines (`lineShift`: lines the old and new spec share at the start and end keep their starts, shifted; changed lines play as new). `resetState` and `beginStream()` clear them. Painting only: `paintBox` works out a pose (`enterPose`: opacity, a rise of `theme.space.md` px, a scale from `ENTER_SCALE` 0.94, eased out over `ENTER_MS` 520) and draws the element and its children through a canvas transform; boxes, hit regions, clips, the text layer and the accessibility tree stay where layout put them, so an element can be pressed mid-entrance. An element with a role draws with its pose instead of the plain fade-in. `stagger=true`: the element's children each take their turn (their own `enter=`, else this element's, else fade), `STAGGER_MS` 80 apart among the children starting in the same frame, at most `MAX_STAGGER_STEPS` 10 steps; the element's own pose then moves only its own drawing, not its children. Sight: an entrance starts at the first frame in which `SIGHT_PX` 24 px of the element (or all of it, if smaller) is inside `viewport()` and the clip of the scrolling element around it; until then it draws invisibly at its starting pose and asks for no frames, and a scroll anywhere in the document (`scroll` in capture) or a window resize (a host growing its frame to fit) repaints while one is waiting. The visible area is read at most once a frame, only when an entrance asks. Where the visible area isn't known (Node, a host without a window) everything is in sight. For components: `PaintContext.progress(box, key, ms, ease)` is a long-running eased 0..1 (a slow zoom) on the same starts and sight (kept by id and key, so it doesn't restart while streaming; 1 at once with motion off). `SayShape.entranceOf(id)` and `sayshape/testing`'s `ui.entrance(query)` give an element's role and progress in the last frame. The guide explains it only with `GuideOptions.motion` (`motionGuide`). Tests `test/entrance.test.ts`. |

| Accessibility | `src/a11y/` | What the screen is to a screen reader ([plan](plans/accessibility.md)). `ComponentDef.semantics` gives an element's role, name and state (`semantics.ts`; `null` = layout only, `false` = hidden, `parts` matched to hit regions by `detail`); `buildAccessibilityTree` (`tree.ts`) gathers them after paint, headless, keyed by element id so hosts can diff; the engine talks only to an `AccessibilityHost` (`host.ts`). ## Limits

Limits: entrance motion staggers at most 10 steps (`MAX_STAGGER_STEPS` in `src/motion.ts`): children past the tenth starting in one frame start with it. a spec derives at most 200 values (`MAX_DERIVED` in `src/parser.ts`). A `select` offers at most 200 options (`MAX_SELECT_OPTIONS`), 8 shown at once. A `table` shows at most 200 rows and 64 columns (`MAX_TABLE_ROWS`, `MAX_TABLE_COLUMNS` in `src/components/table.ts`) and a message instead of more; columns that don't fit scroll sideways. A `chart` draws at most 200 items (`MAX_CHART_ITEMS`), 6 series (`MAX_SERIES`) and 6 pie or donut slices (`MAX_SLICES`; past that, the 5 largest plus an "Other" slice), and says so instead of drawing more, or when it's too narrow for them; a funnel shows at most 12 stages (`MAX_FUNNEL_STAGES` in `src/components/chart-kinds/funnel.ts`) and no negative values; a bullet chart at most 20 rows (`MAX_BULLET_ROWS` in `src/components/chart-kinds/bullet.ts`) and no negative values; a heatmap's cells are at least `MIN_HEATMAP_CELL` (12 px) wide, so a message replaces columns that don't fit; a pie or donut shows a message for negative values or a zero total; horizontal bars stop at `MAX_HBAR_HEIGHT` (1200 px of rows, so fewer rows with more series) and show a message suggesting `take()` instead (`src/components/chart.ts`). A `stat`'s `spark=` takes a list of numbers; `pluck(list, 'field')` takes one from a list of records. Elements may nest at most `MAX_DEPTH` (100) levels (`src/parser.ts`), because layout and paint are recursive. Deeper lines become an error diagnostic and are skipped. Data literals nest at most 20 levels and hold at most 10,000 values (`MAX_DATA_VALUES`); data from the host (`state.set`) may hold up to 1,000,000 values (`MAX_HOST_VALUES`: each row and field counts, so about 100,000 rows of 9 columns) (`src/data.ts`); `each` shows at most 500 items and 10,000 copies per layout (`src/each.ts`). A `schedule` draws at most 200 items (`MAX_SCHEDULE_ITEMS`), 7 days in week view (`MAX_WEEK_DAYS`) and 732 days in a timeline (`MAX_TIMELINE_DAYS`, in `src/components/schedule.ts`), and shows a message instead of more. A `board` draws at most 500 cards (`MAX_BOARD_CARDS`) and 12 stages (`MAX_BOARD_STAGES`, in `src/components/board.ts`). A `tree` draws at most 300 items (`MAX_TREE_NODES`, in `src/components/tree.ts`). A `flow` draws at most 40 steps (`MAX_FLOW_NODES`, in `src/components/flow.ts`). A `menu` lists at most 20 items (`MAX_MENU_ITEMS`, in `src/components/menu.ts`).
